\begin{abstract}
For every ordered pair of populations, BA3 reports a Bayes factor for the
hypothesis of no migration, $H_0\colon m_{ij}=0$, against $H_1\colon m_{ij}>0$,
computed by the Savage--Dickey density ratio. This note gives the model, the
derivation and the implementation used from BA3 3.6.0 onward. Because the
migration rates affect the data only through the ancestry of the sampled
individuals, the posterior density of $m_{ij}$ at zero can be computed exactly
given the sampled ancestries, and averaged over the MCMC sample
(Rao--Blackwellization). The result is
\[
\BF_{01} \;=\; \Pr(c_{ij}=0 \mid \data)\,\frac{n_i+K-1}{K-1},
\]
where $c_{ij}$ is the number of individuals sampled in population $i$ that are
first- or second-generation migrants from $j$, $n_i$ is the number of
individuals sampled in $i$, and $K$ is the number of populations. The estimate
is exact up to Monte Carlo error: there is no bandwidth or threshold to choose.
It shows that the evidence for $H_0$ can never exceed $(n_i+K-1)/(K-1)$, so
it grows with the number of individuals sampled, not with the number of loci.

By default $\Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\data)$ is estimated by the fraction of MCMC states
with no migrant on the route. When migration is strongly supported no state has
$c_{ij}=0$, and only a resolution limit can be printed. The option \texttt{-B}
(Section~\ref{sec:exactbf}) instead averages, over the states, the exact
probability that no sampled individual is a migrant given the state's migration
rates, allele frequencies and inbreeding coefficients. This estimate is positive
on every route, so $\BF_{01}$ is estimated however strong the evidence for
migration. Together the two results show that migration is far easier to detect
than to exclude: a single clearly identified migrant can make the evidence for
migration overwhelming, while the evidence against it is capped by the number of
individuals sampled. Section~\ref{sec:old} describes the kernel-density estimator
used in versions 3.4.0--3.5.0 and the two errors that made it overstate
$\BF_{01}$.
\end{abstract}

\section{The migration model and its prior}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Migration rates and ancestry}

Let $K$ be the number of sampled populations. For a receiving population $i$,
$m_{ij}$ ($j\neq i$) is the fraction of individuals in $i$ that are migrants,
or offspring of migrants, from $j$ within the last two generations, and
$m_{ii} = 1-\sum_{j\neq i} m_{ij}$. An individual sampled in $i$ has one of
$2K-1$ ancestries, with prior probabilities
\begin{align}
\Pr(\text{non-migrant}) &= 1 - 3\textstyle\sum_{j\neq i} m_{ij}, \label{eq:anc0}\\
\Pr(\text{first-generation migrant from } j) &= m_{ij}, \label{eq:anc1}\\
\Pr(\text{second-generation migrant from } j) &= 2\,m_{ij}. \label{eq:anc2}
\end{align}
These probabilities must be non-negative, which requires
\begin{equation}
m_{ij}\ge 0, \qquad \sum_{j\neq i} m_{ij} \le \tfrac13 \quad(\text{equivalently } m_{ii}\ge \tfrac23).
\label{eq:constraint}
\end{equation}

For the $n_i$ individuals sampled in population $i$, let $z$ denote their
ancestries and define the counts
\begin{align*}
n_{i0} &= \text{number of non-migrants},\\
n_{ij1} &= \text{number of first-generation migrants from } j, \quad j\neq i,\\
n_{ij2} &= \text{number of second-generation migrants from } j, \quad j\neq i,
\end{align*}
and the total number of migrants from $j$, of either generation,
\begin{equation}
c_{ij} = n_{ij1} + n_{ij2},
\label{eq:cij}
\end{equation}
so that $n_{i0} + \sum_{j\neq i} c_{ij} = n_i$. The event $c_{ij}=0$ (the
sample from $i$ contains no migrants from $j$) is the sample counterpart of
$H_0\colon m_{ij}=0$ in Section~\ref{sec:sd}. In the source code the counts
are \texttt{migrantCounts[i][i][0]}, \texttt{migrantCounts[i][j][1]} and
\texttt{migrantCounts[i][j][2]}.

\subsection{The prior on migration rates}

The rows of the migration matrix are a priori independent, and each row is
uniform on the region (\ref{eq:constraint}). With the change of variables
\[
u_{ij} = 3\,m_{ij}\quad (j\neq i), \qquad u_{i0} = 1-\sum_{j\neq i} u_{ij},
\]
the region becomes the $(K-1)$-dimensional probability simplex in the $K$
coordinates $(u_{i0}, u_{ij}, j\neq i)$, and the uniform prior is
\begin{equation}
(u_{i0}, u_{ij}, j\neq i) \sim \Dir(1,\ldots,1) \quad\text{over } K \text{ categories}.
\label{eq:dirprior}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Derivation.}
The free coordinates of row $i$ are the $d=K-1$ off-diagonal values
$u_{ij}$, $j\neq i$; $u_{i0}$ is determined by them. The map $m\mapsto u=3m$
has constant Jacobian $3^{d}$, so a prior that is uniform in $m$ on
(\ref{eq:constraint}) is uniform in $u$ on the region
\[
S_d(1) = \Big\{u_{ij}\ge 0,\ \textstyle\sum_{j\neq i}u_{ij}\le 1\Big\}.
\]
A corner simplex $S_d(r)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^d: x_k\ge 0,\ \sum_k x_k\le r\}$
has volume $r^d/d!$. The uniform density on $S_d(1)$ is therefore $d! =
(K-1)!$, which is the $\Dir(1,\ldots,1)$ density over $K$ categories,
$\Gamma(K)/\Gamma(1)^K = (K-1)!$. This proves (\ref{eq:dirprior}).

To obtain the marginal of one coordinate, fix $u_{ij}=u\in[0,1]$. The other
$d-1=K-2$ off-diagonal coordinates range over $S_{K-2}(1-u)$, whose volume is
$(1-u)^{K-2}/(K-2)!$. Integrating them out,
\[
p(u_{ij}=u) = (K-1)!\,\frac{(1-u)^{K-2}}{(K-2)!} = (K-1)(1-u)^{K-2},
\qquad 0\le u\le 1,
\]
which is the $\Beta(1,K-1)$ density. Finally, $m_{ij}=u_{ij}/3$ gives
$p(m_{ij}=m) = 3\,p(u_{ij}=3m)$, so the marginal prior of $m_{ij}$ is
\begin{equation}
p(m_{ij}) = 3(K-1)\,(1-3m_{ij})^{K-2}, \qquad 0\le m_{ij}\le \tfrac13,
\label{eq:marginalprior}
\end{equation}
and setting $m_{ij}=0$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;p(m_{ij}=0) = 3(K-1).\;}
\label{eq:prior0}
\end{equation}
For $K=2$ there is a single off-diagonal rate, $S_{0}$ is a point, and
(\ref{eq:marginalprior}) reduces to the Uniform$(0,\frac13)$ density $3$.
For $K>2$ the marginal piles up toward zero because the $K-1$ off-diagonal rates
share the total of at most $\frac13$.

\paragraph{The prior the sampler targets.}
Equation (\ref{eq:dirprior}) is the prior the MCMC actually samples. The
migration-rate move in \texttt{src/main.cpp} picks a row $i$ and one source
$j$, proposes $m_{ij}' = m_{ij} + \delta(U-\frac12)$ with $U\sim
U(0,1)$, and reflects $m_{ij}'$ into $[0, \frac13 - \sum_{k\neq i,j} m_{ik}]$.
For fixed values of the other rates, this proposal is symmetric. The
Metropolis--Hastings ratio contains only the probability of the ancestry counts
under (\ref{eq:anc0})--(\ref{eq:anc2}), with no prior term. The stationary
distribution is therefore the likelihood times a prior that is flat on
(\ref{eq:constraint}). As a check, running this move with all counts set to
zero (so that the chain samples the prior) gives
$\Pr(m_{ij}<\varepsilon)/\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon=0.002$ equal to $9.0$
for $K=4$ and $26.6$ for $K=10$. The exact values are $8.9$ and $26.4$, and
(\ref{eq:prior0}) gives $9$ and $27$ in the limit.

\subsection{Prior probability of no migrants in the sample}

Each of the $n_i$ individuals is, independently given the migration rates, a
migrant from $j$ (of either generation) with probability
$m_{ij}+2m_{ij}=3m_{ij}=u_{ij}$, so given $u_{ij}$ the probability that none is
a migrant from $j$ is $(1-u_{ij})^{n_i}$. Averaging over the marginal prior
$(K-1)(1-u)^{K-2}$ of $u_{ij}$ derived above,
\begin{equation}
\Pr(c_{ij}=0) = \E\big[(1-u_{ij})^{n_i}\big]
= \int_0^1 (K-1)(1-u)^{n_i+K-2}\,du = \frac{K-1}{n_i+K-1}.
\label{eq:priorc0}
\end{equation}
(More generally, $c_{ij}$ is beta-binomial with parameters $(n_i, 1, K-1)$.)
This is the prior probability that none of the $n_i$ sampled individuals is a
migrant from $j$.

\section{The Savage--Dickey density ratio}
\label{sec:sd}

\subsection{Hypotheses}

For each ordered pair $(i,j)$, $j\neq i$, BA3 tests
\begin{align*}
H_0&\colon m_{ij}=0 \quad\text{(no migration from } j \text{ into } i),\\
H_1&\colon m_{ij}>0,
\end{align*}
where $H_1$ is the full BA3 model. The Bayes factor is
$\BF_{01} = p(\data\mid H_0)/p(\data\mid H_1)$.

\subsection{The identity}

When $H_0$ fixes a parameter at a point inside the parameter space of $H_1$,
and the prior on the remaining parameters under $H_0$ equals their conditional
prior under $H_1$ at that point, then (Dickey, 1971; Verdinelli \& Wasserman,
1995)
\begin{equation}
\BF_{01} = \frac{p(m_{ij}=0 \mid \data, H_1)}{p(m_{ij}=0 \mid H_1)}.
\label{eq:sd}
\end{equation}
The ratio is the posterior density at zero over the prior density at zero, both
under the full model.

\paragraph{The condition holds here.}
Under $H_1$, the conditional prior of the remaining coordinates of row $i$
given $u_{ij}=0$ is $\Dir(1,\ldots,1)$ over the other $K-1$ categories. That
is, it is flat on the reduced region. All other parameters (other rows, allele
frequencies, inbreeding coefficients and, with \texttt{-H}, the ancestral
frequencies and drift parameters) are a priori independent of row $i$.
$H_0$ is therefore the BA3 model with the route $j\to i$ removed and the same
flat prior on everything else, which is the natural null model.

\section{Exact posterior density at zero}
\label{sec:exact}

\subsection{The full conditional of a migration row}

The data $\data$ (genotypes) depend on the migration rates only through the
ancestries $z$: given $z$, the genotype probabilities involve allele
frequencies and inbreeding coefficients but not $m$. Hence
$m \perp \data \mid z$, and the full conditional of row $i$ depends only on the
counts of that row. Under (\ref{eq:anc0})--(\ref{eq:anc2}), the probability of the
ancestries of the $n_i$ individuals is
\[
p(z_i \mid u) \;=\; u_{i0}^{\,n_{i0}}
\prod_{j\neq i} \Big(\frac{u_{ij}}{3}\Big)^{n_{ij1}} \Big(\frac{2u_{ij}}{3}\Big)^{n_{ij2}}
\;=\; u_{i0}^{\,n_{i0}} \prod_{j\neq i} \frac{2^{\,n_{ij2}}}{3^{\,c_{ij}}}\, u_{ij}^{\,c_{ij}}
\;\propto\; u_{i0}^{\,n_{i0}} \prod_{j\neq i} u_{ij}^{\,c_{ij}},
\]
using $u_{i0} = 1-\sum_{j\neq i}u_{ij}$ and (\ref{eq:cij}). Each migrant from
$j$ contributes one factor of $u_{ij}$, whichever its generation. The factor 2
for a second-generation migrant does not involve $u$ and cancels on
normalization, so only the total $c_{ij}$ matters. With the Dirichlet prior
(\ref{eq:dirprior}),
\begin{equation}
(u_{i0}, u_{ij}, j\neq i) \mid z \;\sim\; \Dir\big(n_{i0}+1,\; c_{ij}+1,\; j\neq i\big),
\end{equation}
whose parameters sum to $n_i+K$. The marginal of one coordinate is
\begin{equation}
u_{ij} \mid z \;\sim\; \Beta\big(c_{ij}+1,\; n_i+K-1-c_{ij}\big).
\label{eq:condbeta}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Density at zero}

A $\Beta(a,b)$ density at zero is $b$ if $a=1$ and $0$ if $a>1$. From
(\ref{eq:condbeta}), and multiplying by $3$ for $m_{ij}=u_{ij}/3$,
\begin{equation}
p(m_{ij}=0 \mid z) =
\begin{cases}
3\,(n_i+K-1) & \text{if } c_{ij}=0,\\
0 & \text{if } c_{ij}\ge 1.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:cond0}
\end{equation}
If even one sampled individual is a migrant from $j$, a migration rate of
exactly zero is impossible. If none is, the density at zero is the largest
value a $\Beta(1,\cdot)$ density can take for this sample size.

\subsection{Rao--Blackwellization}

The posterior density at zero is the posterior expectation of the conditional
density (Gelfand \& Smith, 1990):
\begin{equation}
p(m_{ij}=0 \mid \data) = \E\big[p(m_{ij}=0\mid z)\,\big|\,\data\big]
= 3\,(n_i+K-1)\,\Pr(c_{ij}=0 \mid \data).
\label{eq:post0}
\end{equation}
Dividing by the prior density (\ref{eq:prior0}) gives the Bayes factor. The
factor $3$ from the Jacobian of $m=u/3$ cancels.

\begin{proposition}
Under the BA3 model,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\BF_{01} = \Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\data)\,\frac{n_i+K-1}{K-1}
= \frac{\Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\data)}{\Pr(c_{ij}=0)}.\;}
\label{eq:bf}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The second form, from (\ref{eq:priorc0}), shows that $\BF_{01}$ is the ratio of
posterior to prior probability that the sample from $i$ contains no migrants
from $j$.

\section{Properties}
\label{sec:props}

\paragraph{Uninformative data.}
If the genotypes carry no information about ancestry, then
$\Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\data)=\Pr(c_{ij}=0)$ and $\BF_{01}=1$.

\paragraph{Evidence for no migration is bounded.}
Since $\Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\data)\le 1$,
\begin{equation}
\BF_{01} \le \frac{n_i+K-1}{K-1}.
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
The bound is reached when the data establish with certainty that no sampled
individual is a migrant from $j$. It cannot be exceeded however many loci are
typed: perfect knowledge of the ancestry of $n_i$ individuals still cannot
exclude a small positive $m_{ij}$. More loci bring $\Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\data)$
closer to 0 or 1, and only more individuals raise the ceiling.
Table~\ref{tab:bound} gives values. On the Jeffreys scale
(Table~\ref{tab:jeffreys}), ``strong'' evidence for $H_0$ ($\BF_{01}>10$)
needs $n_i > 9(K-1)$, and ``decisive'' evidence ($\BF_{01}>100$) needs
$n_i > 99(K-1)$.

\paragraph{Evidence for migration is unbounded.}
$\BF_{01}\to 0$ as $\Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\data)\to 0$, so the evidence for
migration can be arbitrarily strong. The default estimator cannot measure it
once $\Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\data)$ is below about $1/N$ for $N$ recorded states
(Section~\ref{sec:mc}); the estimator of Section~\ref{sec:exactbf}
(\texttt{-B}) can.

\paragraph{Migration is easier to detect than to exclude.}
The two previous properties are asymmetric. Evidence for migration on a route
needs only one sampled individual whose genotypes clearly place it as a migrant
or the offspring of one; evidence against migration needs many individuals, none
of which is a migrant, and is capped by (\ref{eq:bound}) however many loci are
typed. In the Malagasy palm data set BN (52 individuals from 8 populations,
6--7 each, 5,524 SNPs; \texttt{-c -H}), no absent route can exceed
$\BF_{01}=(7+7)/7=2.0$, and all 53 are ``Weak for $H_0$''. One individual in
population BN4, with probability 1 a second-generation migrant from BN3, gives
$\log_{10}\BF_{01}\approx -176$ for that route with \texttt{-B}, although $m$ is
barely estimated ($0.05\pm0.03$). Decisive evidence for migration is evidence
under the BA3 model: in the same data, 6 of the 7 individuals in BN1 are
assigned as second-generation migrants from BN2 ($\log_{10}\BF_{01}\approx
-1031$), a pattern that can also arise when two samples are not distinct
populations.

\paragraph{Relation to interval-null estimators.}
A common way to estimate (\ref{eq:sd}) from MCMC output is to replace the point
null by a small interval:
\[
\BF_{01} \approx \frac{\Pr(m_{ij}<\varepsilon\mid\data)}{\Pr(m_{ij}<\varepsilon)}.
\]
This is the Bayes factor for $m_{ij}<\varepsilon$ against the full model, and it
converges to (\ref{eq:sd}) as $\varepsilon\to 0$. Given $z$, both terms are
known exactly: $\Pr(m_{ij}<\varepsilon\mid z) = 1-(1-3\varepsilon)^{n_i+K-1}$
when $c_{ij}=0$, and $\Pr(m_{ij}<\varepsilon) = 1-(1-3\varepsilon)^{K-1}$.
Equation (\ref{eq:bf}) is the $\varepsilon\to 0$ limit taken analytically, so
it has neither the bias of a finite $\varepsilon$ nor the extra variance of
counting raw samples of $m_{ij}$ near zero. As a check, the BA3 migration move
run with fixed counts ($n_i=50$, $K=10$, $c_{ij}=0$) gave
$\Pr(m_{ij}<0.001)/0.001 = 162$, equal to the exact finite-$\varepsilon$ value
$162$, which tends to $3(n_i+K-1)=177$ as $\varepsilon\to 0$.

\paragraph{Model misspecification.}
The test is exact for the BA3 model. It inherits that model's failures. In
particular, if two samples come from one randomly mating population, BA3 can
only explain their shared allele frequencies by migration, so $c_{ij}>0$ in
every sampled state and $\BF_{01}$ is reported as decisive for migration in
both directions (see the manual, ``Populations that should be merged'').

\section{Monte Carlo estimation}
\label{sec:mc}

\subsection{Estimator}

After burn-in, every $s$ iterations (the sampling interval \texttt{-n}), BA3
records for each route whether $c_{ij}=0$ in the current state. With $N$
recorded states $z^{(1)},\ldots,z^{(N)}$,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Pr}(c_{ij}=0\mid\data) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=1}^N \1\big[c_{ij}^{(t)}=0\big],
\qquad
\widehat{\BF}_{01} = \widehat{\Pr}(c_{ij}=0\mid\data)\,\frac{n_i+K-1}{K-1}.
\end{equation}
The Monte Carlo standard error of $\widehat{\Pr}$ is approximately
$\sqrt{\hat p(1-\hat p)/N_{\text{eff}}}$, where $N_{\text{eff}}$ is the
effective sample size of the indicator series. Its relative error is largest
when $\hat p$ is small, which is when the evidence for migration is strong.

\subsection{When no recorded state has $c_{ij}=0$}

If $\widehat{\Pr}=0$, the estimate of $\BF_{01}$ would be $0$. BA3 instead
reports
\[
\BF_{01} < \frac{1}{N}\,\frac{n_i+K-1}{K-1},
\]
the value that a single recorded state with $c_{ij}=0$ would give, marked with
\texttt{<}. The interpretation gets the suffix ``or more'' (for example,
``Strong for $H_1$ or more'') unless it is already ``Decisive for $H_1$''. This
is a reporting convention, not a guaranteed bound: it says only that the run
was too short to resolve values below about $1/N$. Even for $N$ independent
states, zero occurrences give $\Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\data) < 1/N$ only with about
63\% confidence ($1-e^{-1}$); a 95\% limit is about $3/N$. The printed value,
and with it the interpretation, therefore depends on the run length and the
sampling interval, not on the data. The estimator of Section~\ref{sec:exactbf}
(option \texttt{-B}) gives a positive estimate on such routes.

\subsection{Other details}

Routes with fewer than 100 recorded states are reported as ``Insufficient
samples''. The estimate depends on the sampler mixing between states with
$c_{ij}=0$ and $c_{ij}>0$. A route whose indicator never or rarely changes
during a run, or whose $\widehat{\Pr}$ differs between seeds, should be
treated with caution. The collapsed sampler (\texttt{-c}) and the hierarchical
allele-frequency prior (\texttt{-H}) change how the ancestries $z$ are updated
and what prior the allele frequencies have, but not the prior on $m$ or the
migration move, so (\ref{eq:bf}) applies unchanged.

\section{Exact estimates for strongly supported routes (\texttt{-B})}
\label{sec:exactbf}

\subsection{The estimator}

The counting estimator of Section~\ref{sec:mc} averages the indicator
$\1[c_{ij}=0]$ over the recorded states. When migration on a route is strongly
supported, $\Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\data)$ is far below $1/N$ and the indicator is
almost always zero. A second application of Rao--Blackwellization removes this
limitation. Let $\phi$ denote the migration rates $m$, the allele frequencies
$p$ and the inbreeding coefficients $F$. Given $\phi$ and the data, the
ancestries of different individuals are independent: individual $k$, sampled
in population $i$, has ancestry $(s,g)$ with probability
\[
\Pr(z_k=(s,g)\mid\phi,\data) \;\propto\; \pi_i(s,g)\,\Pr(G_k\mid s,g,p,F),
\]
where $\pi_i(i,0)=1-3\sum_{j\neq i}m_{ij}$, $\pi_i(s,1)=m_{is}$ and
$\pi_i(s,2)=2m_{is}$ (equations (\ref{eq:anc0})--(\ref{eq:anc2})), and
$\Pr(G_k\mid\cdot)$ is the BA3 genotype probability over the observed loci
(missing genotypes are omitted, which marginalizes them exactly). With
$q_{kj} = \Pr(z_k=(j,1)\mid\phi,\data)+\Pr(z_k=(j,2)\mid\phi,\data)$,
\begin{equation}
\Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\phi,\data) = \prod_{k:\,\text{sampled in } i} (1-q_{kj}),
\qquad
\Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\data) = \E\Big[\prod_k (1-q_{kj})\,\Big|\,\data\Big].
\label{eq:rbprod}
\end{equation}
With $T$ evaluated states $\phi^{(1)},\ldots,\phi^{(T)}$ and
$w^{(t)}_{ij} = \prod_k (1-q^{(t)}_{kj})$,
\[
\widetilde{\Pr}(c_{ij}=0\mid\data) = \frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T w^{(t)}_{ij},
\qquad
\widetilde{\BF}_{01} = \widetilde{\Pr}(c_{ij}=0\mid\data)\,\frac{n_i+K-1}{K-1}.
\]
Every $w^{(t)}_{ij}$ is positive, so the estimate is positive even when no
recorded state has $c_{ij}=0$. Both estimators are unbiased for the same
quantity; the new one replaces each indicator by its conditional expectation
given $\phi$, which cannot increase the variance.

The product in (\ref{eq:rbprod}) must be formed state by state. Using instead the
posterior ancestry probabilities averaged over the run (the
\texttt{BA3indiv.txt} values) ignores the dependence between individuals that
runs through $m$: in a simulated data set with 15 individuals per population it
understated $\Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\data)$ about 20-fold on a true route.

\subsection{The collapsed sampler}

With \texttt{-c} the allele frequencies are not part of the state. Given the
ancestries and the latent identity-by-descent and phase codes, the gene-copy
counts $n^{(t)}_{sla}$ are sufficient, and the conditional distribution of
$p_{sl}$ is $\mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha_{sla}+n^{(t)}_{sla})$, with
$\alpha_{sla}=1$ under the flat prior and $\alpha_{sla}=c_s\pi_{la}$ under
\texttt{-H}. At each evaluation BA3 draws $p^{(t)}$ from this distribution, so
that $(z^{(t)},p^{(t)},m^{(t)},F^{(t)})$ is a draw from the joint posterior, and
then evaluates $w^{(t)}_{ij}$. The draws use a separate random-number stream, so
\texttt{-B} does not change the chain or any other output.

\subsection{Computation}

At each evaluation BA3 computes, for every individual and every one of the
$2K-1$ ancestries, the probability of its genotypes. Per population, locus and
allele it first tabulates $p_{sla}$ and the homozygote probability
$F_sp_{sla}+(1-F_s)p_{sla}^2$; heterozygotes of non-migrant and first-generation
ancestry have probability $2(1-F_s)p_{sla}p_{slb}$, and second-generation
ancestry from $s$ has $p_{sla}p_{ila}$ for a homozygote and
$p_{sla}p_{ilb}+p_{slb}p_{ila}$ for a heterozygote. These are multiplied across
loci on the probability scale, with the running product moved to a log offset
before it can underflow, so most loci need no logarithm. Then
$\log(1-q_{kj})$ is computed as the log-sum-exp over the ancestries not from $j$
minus the log-sum-exp over all ancestries, which stays accurate when $q_{kj}$ is
close to 1, and the averages over states are accumulated in log space. With
4,383 individuals, 2,000 loci and 8 populations one evaluation takes about
0.14\,s, so \texttt{-B}~$K$ evaluates only every $K$-th recorded state.

\subsection{Monte Carlo error}

No standard error is reported. When $\Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\data)$ is extremely small,
$w^{(t)}_{ij}$ varies over many orders of magnitude and a few states dominate the
average. The usual estimate of the relative standard error,
$\sqrt{(\overline{w^2}/\bar w^{\,2}-1)/T}$, then approaches 1 whatever the data,
because $\overline{w^2}/\bar w^{\,2}\approx T$ when one term dominates; and it
assumes the evaluated states are independent. In this regime $\log_{10}\BF_{01}$
varies between runs in its last digits ($-9407$ and $-9485$ for two seeds on a
simulated data set with 4,383 individuals) while being far beyond any decision
threshold. Where the value is close to a threshold, $w^{(t)}_{ij}$ varies much
less, and its precision is best judged by comparing runs with different seeds.

\subsection{Validation}

On the small exact data sets of Section~\ref{sec:validation}, the estimate agrees
with the exact $\Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\data)$ for the standard sampler, \texttt{-c}
and \texttt{-c -H} (16 + 4 comparisons, four seeds each, largest $|z|=2.3$). On a
simulated data set with 15 individuals per population and $F_{\mathrm{ST}}=0.01$
it agrees with the counting estimator on every route where that is nonzero (for
example 0.0259 and 0.0255 on a true route), and on a true route where no
recorded state had $c_{ij}=0$ it gives $\log_{10}\BF_{01}\approx -14.6$ and
$-15.0$ for two seeds, where the counting estimator could only report
$<0.001$.

\section{Kullback--Leibler information gain}
\label{sec:kl}

The \texttt{KL(bits)} column reports
\[
\KL\big(p(m_{ij}\mid\data)\,\|\,p(m_{ij})\big)
= \int_0^{1/3} p(m_{ij}\mid\data)\,\log_2\frac{p(m_{ij}\mid\data)}{p(m_{ij})}\,dm_{ij},
\]
the information gained about $m_{ij}$, with the exact marginal prior
(\ref{eq:marginalprior}). The posterior density is estimated from the stored
samples of $m_{ij}$ by a Gaussian kernel density estimate, reflected at $0$:
\[
\hat p(m\mid\data) = \frac{1}{Nh\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{t=1}^N
\left[\exp\!\Big(-\frac{(m-m^{(t)})^2}{2h^2}\Big) + \exp\!\Big(-\frac{(m+m^{(t)})^2}{2h^2}\Big)\right],
\]
with Silverman's bandwidth $h = 1.06\,\hat\sigma\,N^{-1/5}$, where
$\hat\sigma$ is the posterior standard deviation of $m_{ij}$. If $h<10^{-6}$,
$h=0.01$ is used instead. The integral is approximated by the midpoint rule on
$G=1000$ intervals of $[0,\frac13]$. Grid points where
$\hat p < 10^{-10}$ are skipped, and the result is converted to bits and
truncated at $0$. The KL statistic is descriptive only and is not used in the
Bayes factor. Unlike $\BF_{01}$, it depends on a bandwidth and on how well a
kernel estimate represents a posterior piled up against $0$.

\section{Algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Savage--Dickey Bayes factors and KL information gain}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\For{each route $(i,j)$, $j\neq i$}
  \State $\text{sumM}\gets 0$, $\text{sumM2}\gets 0$, $\text{countZero}\gets 0$, $N\gets 0$, samples $\gets$ empty
\EndFor
\For{each iteration $t > \text{burn-in}$ with $t \bmod s = 0$}
  \For{each route $(i,j)$, $j\neq i$}
    \State $m \gets m_{ij}$; $\text{sumM}\gets\text{sumM}+m$; $\text{sumM2}\gets\text{sumM2}+m^2$; append $m$ to samples
    \If{\texttt{migrantCounts[i][j][1]} $+$ \texttt{migrantCounts[i][j][2]} $=0$}
      \State $\text{countZero}\gets\text{countZero}+1$
    \EndIf
    \State $N\gets N+1$
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\For{each destination $i$}
  \State $n_i \gets \sum_{j}\sum_{a=0}^{2}$ \texttt{migrantCounts[i][j][a]} \Comment{individuals sampled in $i$}
  \State $B_i \gets (n_i+K-1)/(K-1)$ \Comment{largest attainable $\BF_{01}$}
  \For{each source $j\neq i$}
    \If{$N<100$} \State report ``Insufficient samples''; \textbf{continue} \EndIf
    \State $\hat p \gets \text{countZero}/N$
    \If{$\text{countZero}=0$} \State $\BF_{01}\gets B_i/N$, marked ``$<$''
    \Else \State $\BF_{01}\gets \hat p\, B_i$ \EndIf
    \State $\hat\sigma\gets\sqrt{\text{sumM2}/N-(\text{sumM}/N)^2}$; $h\gets 1.06\,\hat\sigma\,N^{-1/5}$; \textbf{if} $h<10^{-6}$ \textbf{then} $h\gets 0.01$
    \State $\KL \gets \sum_{g=0}^{G-1} \hat p(m_g)\log_2\!\big(\hat p(m_g)/p(m_g)\big)\,\Delta m$, $m_g=(g+\frac12)\Delta m$, $\Delta m=\frac{1}{3G}$, $p(m)$ from (\ref{eq:marginalprior})
    \State report mean, SD, $\hat p$, $\BF_{01}$, $\log_{10}\BF_{01}$, $\max(\KL,0)$, interpretation
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Exact Bayes factors (\texttt{-B}~$K$), one evaluation}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\If{collapsed sampler}
  \State draw $p_{sl}\sim\mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha_{sla}+n_{sla})$ for every population $s$ and locus $l$ \Comment{own RNG stream}
\Else
  \State take $p$ from the current state
\EndIf
\State tabulate $p_{sla}$ and $F_sp_{sla}+(1-F_s)p_{sla}^2$ for every $s,l,a$
\For{each individual $k$, sampled in population $i$}
  \State $\ell^{\mathrm{pure}}_s \gets \log\Pr(G_k\mid\text{both genes from } s, F_s)$ for every $s$ \Comment{observed loci only}
  \State $\ell^{\mathrm{hyb}}_s \gets \log\Pr(G_k\mid\text{one gene from } s\text{, one from } i)$ for every $s\neq i$
  \State $c_0 \gets \log(1-3\textstyle\sum_{j\neq i} m_{ij}) + \ell^{\mathrm{pure}}_i$
  \State $c_{1s} \gets \log m_{is}+\ell^{\mathrm{pure}}_s$ and $c_{2s}\gets\log 2m_{is}+\ell^{\mathrm{hyb}}_s$ for every $s\neq i$
  \State $T \gets \mathrm{logsumexp}(c_0, c_{1s}, c_{2s}: s\neq i)$
  \For{each source $j\neq i$}
    \State $A_{ij} \gets A_{ij} + \mathrm{logsumexp}(c_0, c_{1s}, c_{2s}: s\neq i,j) - T$ \Comment{$\log(1-q_{kj})$}
  \EndFor
\EndFor
\State for every route: $S_{ij}\gets\mathrm{logsumexp}(S_{ij}, A_{ij})$; reset $A_{ij}\gets 0$; $T_{\mathrm{eval}}\gets T_{\mathrm{eval}}+1$
\State \textbf{at the end:} $\log\widetilde{\Pr}(c_{ij}=0\mid\data) = S_{ij}-\log T_{\mathrm{eval}}$; $\log\widetilde{\BF}_{01} = \log\widetilde{\Pr} + \log\frac{n_i+K-1}{K-1}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Implementation}

The code is in \texttt{src/main.cpp} (functions \texttt{initSavageDickeyStats},
\texttt{updateSavageDickeyStats}, \texttt{computeKDEatPoint},
\texttt{computeKLDivergence} and \texttt{computeSavageDickeyBayesFactors}), with
the statistics held in
\begin{verbatim}
struct SavageDickeyStats {
    double sumM;                 // running sum of m_ij (mean)
    double sumM2;                // running sum of m_ij^2 (SD)
    long int countZero;          // recorded states with c_ij = 0
    long int nSamples;           // recorded states N
    std::vector<double> samples; // m_ij samples, for the KL estimate
};
\end{verbatim}
\texttt{updateSavageDickeyStats} is called in the sampling block of the main
loop, after burn-in and every \texttt{-n} iterations, with the current
migration rates and ancestry counts. Storing the samples for the KL estimate
needs $O(NK^2)$ memory. The Bayes factor itself needs only the counter
\texttt{countZero}.

The \texttt{-B} estimator is in \texttt{struct ExactBF} and the functions
\texttt{exactBFInit}, \texttt{exactBFStep}, \texttt{exactBFReport} and
\texttt{exactBFFree}. \texttt{exactBFStep} is called next to
\texttt{updateSavageDickeyStats}, on every $K$-th recorded state. The structure
holds, per route, the log of the running sum of $w^{(t)}_{ij}$ and the
current evaluation's $\sum_k\log(1-q_{kj})$; a mask of observed genotypes; the
allele and homozygote probabilities of the current evaluation, stored
contiguously by population, locus and allele; and its own random-number
generator, seeded from the run's seed.

\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

\texttt{tests/exact\_posterior.py} computes the exact BA3 posterior for tiny data
sets by enumerating all $(2K-1)^{N}$ ancestry assignments, with $m$, the allele
frequencies and $F$ integrated out. It reports the exact
$\Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\data)$ and $\BF_{01}$ from (\ref{eq:bf});
\texttt{tests/exact\_posterior\_hier.py} does the same under \texttt{-H}.
\texttt{tests/check\_exact.py} runs BA3 with several seeds and compares the
reported \texttt{Pr(c=0)} and \texttt{BF\_01} (and all other posterior summaries)
with the exact values by $z$-scores. When the change was made, the standard
sampler, \texttt{-c} and \texttt{-c -H} all passed (largest $|z|=2.1$; commit
\texttt{52be5a1}). The test
compares BA3's estimate with the exact $\Pr(c_{ij}=0\mid\data)$, so it checks
the sampling of ancestries and the reported numbers. The derivation of
(\ref{eq:bf}) from (\ref{eq:sd}) is checked by the two simulations of
Sections~\ref{sec:model} and~\ref{sec:props}, which run BA3's migration move
directly. The \texttt{-B} estimator was checked against the same exact values
(Section~\ref{sec:exactbf}); with \texttt{-B} off, the output of the standard
sampler is byte-identical to 3.6.1 (\texttt{tests/regress\_default.sh}, 21 of 21).

%======================================================================
\section{Versions 3.4.0--3.5.0 (superseded)}
\label{sec:old}

Versions 3.4.0 to 3.5.0 estimated the posterior density at zero from the
samples of $m_{ij}$ by a Gaussian kernel density estimate, reflected at $0$,
and divided it by a prior density of $3$. The method has two errors.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Inconsistent bandwidth.} The kernel sum
$S=\sum_t \exp\!\big(-(m^{(t)})^2/2h_{\text{ref}}^2\big)$ was accumulated during
the run with a fixed $h_{\text{ref}}=0.02$, but normalized at the end with
Silverman's bandwidth $h_{\text{opt}}$:
$\hat p(0\mid\data)=2S/(N h_{\text{opt}}\sqrt{2\pi})$. When the posterior is
concentrated near zero, $h_{\text{opt}}\ll h_{\text{ref}}$ and the density at
zero is overstated by roughly $h_{\text{ref}}/h_{\text{opt}}$. A histogram
estimate, the fraction of samples with $m_{ij}<0.005$ divided by $0.005$, was
substituted only when the two differed by more than a factor of 100.

\item \textbf{Wrong prior density.} The prior density at zero was taken as $3$,
as if each $m_{ij}$ were Uniform$(0,\frac13)$. The prior that BA3 samples is
(\ref{eq:dirprior}), with density $3(K-1)$ at zero (\ref{eq:prior0}). For $K>2$
this overstated $\BF_{01}$ by a further factor of $K-1$, and the histogram
fallback had the same error.
\end{enumerate}

Together these overstated $\BF_{01}$ by a factor of about 30 in a
10-population example (commit \texttt{52be5a1}).
Evidence for $H_0$ reported by these versions, especially ``Strong'' or
``Decisive'' for no migration, should be recomputed with BA3 3.6.0 or later.
The KL column of these versions used the same Uniform$(0,\frac13)$ prior.
Sampling was not affected: with the Bayes-factor table excluded, 3.6.0 output
is byte-identical to 3.5.0 for the same seed (\texttt{tests/regress\_default.sh
--no-sd}).
